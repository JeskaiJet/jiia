<Section title="Why Travel Booking Needs a Relationship Layer">
<WideImage src="/images/treeing/banner.png" alt="Treeing product overview" width={3840} height={2160} />

Traditional travel booking platforms usually start with inventory. Guests enter a destination, dates, and party size, then keep narrowing the results through price, ratings, location, and amenities. This model works for first-time exploration, but it is not always the best fit for repeat booking.

In real travel decisions, trust is often not built from zero. A Guest may remember a stay that worked well, a Host who communicated clearly, or a property managed by someone they already trust. But on most platforms, that trust rarely becomes a stable entry point. The Guest still has to search again next time, and the Host has no lightweight way to turn satisfied past guests into a durable customer group.

Treeing's product opportunity starts here: if the platform can structure the trust that already exists between Hosts and Returning Guests, travel booking can become more than a one-off search transaction. It can become a product system built around relationships, repeat booking, and operational efficiency.
</Section>

<Section title="Turning Repeat-Guest Relationships into Shared Value">
Treeing's business logic is built around value for three sides.

For Guests, the platform reduces repeat decision-making cost. Instead of comparing unfamiliar listings from scratch every time, Guests can return to Hosts they already trust, browse new listings, check availability, and potentially access returning-guest benefits.

For Hosts, Treeing reduces the cost of repeat-guest operations. Many Hosts already have returning guests from different platforms or offline channels, but those relationships are often scattered across messages, email, social apps, and platform dashboards. Guest List turns these relationships into a manageable customer group that Hosts can maintain through invitations, dedicated visibility, and operational tools.

For the platform, Treeing's value is not only listing inventory. It is the relationship network. The more stable the Guest-Host relationship becomes, the more the platform can move from one-time transactions toward retention and repeat booking.

<Section title="From Search Pressure to Operational Burden">
When mapping the Guest and Host experience, I separated the problem into two types: one that happens before booking, and one that appears during long-term operations.

On the Guest side, the main issues are search pressure and trust transfer. Travel booking pages often ask users to process destination, price, dates, party size, map, amenities, and reviews at the same time. More information does not always make the decision easier. On mobile, desktop-style filtering is compressed into a small screen, which makes users move back and forth between multiple conditions.

On the Host side, the problem is relationship management and maintenance cost. Multi-listing Hosts need to manage pricing, calendars, availability, and booking sync across different contexts. If Treeing simply added another dashboard, Host adoption cost would be high. To make Treeing worth using, the product needed to reduce duplicated work instead of creating another layer of maintenance.

These observations led me to define Treeing's core product question: how might Treeing reduce the Guest's trust decision cost while also reducing the Host's repeat-booking operational cost?

<Section title="Core Hypothesis: Make Relationships the Booking Entry Point">
Based on the early problem framing, I summarized Treeing's product hypothesis:

If Treeing can turn the trust relationship between Hosts and returning guests into a discoverable, manageable, and bookable product structure, Guests can complete trusted bookings faster, while Hosts can maintain repeat-guest relationships with less effort.

This hypothesis became five design principles: organize the experience around Hosts, Guests, and trust relationships; avoid asking Hosts to abandon existing channels; make Host tools clarify status and speed up maintenance; let each mobile step handle one key decision; and make Host reputation, activity, and relationship entry points visible to Guests.

<Section title="V1: Validating Whether Repeat-Guest Relationships Can Become a Booking Entry Point">
<WideImage src="/images/treeing/guest_list_invitation.png" alt="Treeing Guest List invitation flow" width={2880} height={2048} />

The first version of Treeing did not try to cover the full scope of a travel booking marketplace. V1 focused on a more specific question: when a Guest and a Host already have trust, can the platform turn that relationship into a shorter and more natural booking path?

The V1 design priorities centered on three areas.

First, Guests needed an entry point back to trusted Hosts. My Host and Host Page let users continue browsing around people they already trust instead of starting from generic search.

Second, Hosts needed a basic model for managing returning guests. Guest List organizes scattered guest relationships into a customer group that can be maintained inside the platform, while the invitation flow explains the value of joining the relationship network.

Third, the Host side had to stay lightweight. Dashboard, Listing Editing, and calendar-related tools needed to support daily operations without turning Treeing into another complicated backend.
</Section>

<Section title="Reducing Guest Search Pressure">
On the Guest side, I focused on search pressure. Traditional search asks users to define destination, dates, budget, room type, and preferences upfront, but many travel decisions do not start that way. Users may simply want to see promising options first, then narrow the decision gradually.

Spotlight was designed as a lightweight recommendation queue. It does not require users to complete a full set of filters first. Instead, users express preferences through browsing and swiping. Each interaction is both exploration and feedback.

Mobile Search uses a step-by-step flow instead of compressing a desktop search bar into a phone layout. Destination, dates, and party size are split into separate pages or modal layers, so users handle one question at a time. This makes the search flow clearer on small screens and reduces mistaken selections and repeated confirmation.

<Section title="Productizing Repeat-Guest Relationships">
Treeing's core is not Guest List as a single feature. It is the relationship layer built around it.

For Hosts, Guest List is a relationship management model. It turns returning guests from scattered contacts across channels into a manageable customer group, so Hosts can maintain these relationships instead of reacquiring the same guests every time.

For Guests, the relationship needs to become visible after joining Guest List. My Host provides a long-term entry point for returning to trusted Hosts. Host Page expands the Host from a person behind a single listing into a destination that Guests can follow, understand, and revisit.

The invitation flow is important here. Users are not simply asked to join a list. They first see the value of joining Guest List: easier return visits to trusted Hosts, potential access to better prices or dedicated content, and less search cost for the next booking.

<Section title="Reducing Host Operational Cost">
If Treeing improved only the Guest experience without reducing Host maintenance cost, the platform would be difficult to sustain. Whether Hosts continue using Treeing depends on how well it fits into their existing operational workflow.

Host Dashboard was designed as a daily workspace, not just a data display page. Key business signals, booking status, and schedule information need to be visible as soon as the Host enters the page. When Hosts need to plan further, the calendar can expand into a more complete operational view.

Listing Editing uses a direct-editing approach closer to the Guest view. Hosts do not need to update content inside abstract backend forms while guessing how the final page will look. Instead, they edit within a structure that is close to the final listing page, reducing the mental distance between settings and presentation.

Batch Set and Calendar Connect address multi-listing and multi-platform operations. Batch Set turns repeated price and availability adjustments into batch rules. Calendar Connect lowers adoption cost by syncing with external platforms. Treeing's goal is not to make Hosts abandon existing channels, but to become an easier relationship layer for repeat booking.

<Section title="From a Feature Set to a Relationship-based Booking System">
<WideImage src="/images/treeing/main_page.png" alt="Treeing web main page" width={2880} height={2048} />

The final Treeing design is not a collection of separate features. It is a product system that operates around relationships.

The Guest side supports discovery and return visits. Spotlight reduces early exploration pressure, Mobile Search clarifies mobile decision-making, and My Host and Host Page turn trusted Hosts into returnable entry points.

The Host side supports relationship management and operational maintenance. Guest List manages returning-guest relationships. Dashboard provides a daily operating view. Listing Editing reduces the effort of understanding edits. Batch Set and Calendar Connect handle multi-listing and multi-platform maintenance.

When these features connect, Treeing's core logic becomes clear: Guests can return to trusted Hosts more easily, Hosts can maintain returning-guest relationships with less effort, and the platform can move from one-time transactions toward long-term repeat booking.
</Section>

<Section title="What Treeing Solves">
Treeing's design value can be understood from the Guest, Host, and platform perspectives.

For Guests, Treeing reduces the cost of starting every trip from zero. Through My Host, Host Page, and Spotlight, users can move between trusted relationships and lightweight exploration instead of being forced into complex filtering upfront.

For Hosts, Treeing turns returning-guest relationships into a manageable asset. Guest List helps Hosts maintain long-term customer groups. Batch Set and Calendar Connect reduce multi-listing and multi-platform maintenance cost. Listing Editing makes listing updates closer to the final presentation.

For the platform, Treeing extends value from listing inventory to the relationship network. The platform does not only complete a booking. It helps Hosts and Guests build connections that can be revisited, repeated, and maintained over time.

<Section title="Travel Platforms Are Not Only Search">
Treeing changed how I understand the design focus of travel booking products. At first, the project looked like a listing marketplace, but the real complexity was not showing more listings. It was deciding what the platform should build long-term value around.

If viewed only from the Guest side, the problem looks like search experience. If viewed only from the Host side, it becomes backend efficiency. Treeing's key insight is that these two problems are two ends of the same system. Guests need a more trusted return path, and Hosts need lower-cost relationship maintenance tools. Only when both sides work together can repeat-guest relationships become platform value.

In the end, I focused the design on how relationships can become a booking entry point, rather than only optimizing individual screens. This also made the product lesson clearer: product design is not just about making a flow smoother. It is about finding the point where business model, user behavior, and operational efficiency can form a closed loop.
